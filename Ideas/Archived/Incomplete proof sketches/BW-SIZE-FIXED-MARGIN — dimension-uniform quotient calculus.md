## 1. Operator-by-Frobenius calculus

For compatible matrices,

\[
\|XYZ\|_F
\le\|X\|_{\rm op}\|Y\|_F\|Z\|_{\rm op}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

This is the only multiplication estimate used. If
\(\alpha I\preceq A\preceq\beta I\), then

\[
\|A^{\pm1/2}\|_{\rm op}
\le\max\{\sqrt\beta,\alpha^{-1/2}\},
\qquad
\|\mathcal L_A^{-1}\|_{F\to F}\le(2\alpha)^{-1}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The Fréchet derivatives of inversion have the exact permutation formula

\[
D^k(A^{-1})[H_1,\ldots,H_k]
=(-1)^k\sum_{\sigma\in S_k}
A^{-1}H_{\sigma(1)}A^{-1}\cdots
H_{\sigma(k)}A^{-1},
\tag{1.3}
\]

so

\[
\|D^k(A^{-1})\|_{F^k\to F}
\le k!\alpha^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

The square-root, inverse-square-root, and polar derivative bounds already proved in the BW dossier follow from their resolvent/divided-difference formulas. On the fixed margins they have the common coarse bound

\[
\max_{j\le k_0}
\{\|D^jA^{1/2}\|,\|D^jA^{-1/2}\|,
\|D^j\operatorname{polar}(C)\|\}
\le c_{k_0}\Lambda^{e_{k_0}}.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Every use of a lift \(L\) pays only
\(\|L\|_{\rm op}\le\sqrt\beta\), never
\(\|L\|_F\asymp\sqrt m\).

## 2. Horizontal projector

Let \(G=L^TL\), and let \(\mathscr S_G(\Omega)=G\Omega+\Omega G\). For arbitrary \(Z\),

\[
P_L^{\mathcal H}Z=Z-L\Omega,
\qquad
\mathscr S_G(\Omega)=L^TZ-Z^TL.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Because \(G\succeq\alpha I\),

\[
\|\mathscr S_G^{-1}\|_{F\to F}\le(2\alpha)^{-1}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\|\Omega\|_F
\le{\sqrt\beta\over\alpha}\|Z\|_F,
\qquad
\|P_L^{\mathcal H}Z\|_F
\le\left(1+{\beta\over\alpha}\right)\|Z\|_F.
\tag{2.3}
\]

For parameter directions \(H_1,\ldots,H_k\), differentiate (2.1):

\[
\mathscr S_G(D^k\Omega)
=D^kK
-\sum_{\substack{I\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}\\I\ne\varnothing}}
c_I\,
\mathscr S_{D^{|I|}G[H_I]}
\bigl(D^{k-|I|}\Omega[H_{I^c}]\bigr),
\tag{2.4}
\]

where \(K=L^TZ-Z^TL\), \(D^jG=0\) for \(j>2\), \(D^jK=0\) for \(j>2\), and \(c_I\) are universal combinatorial integers. Apply (2.2), (1.1), and induction. For every fixed \(k\le k_0\),

\[
\|D^kP_L^{\mathcal H}\|_{F^{k+1}\to F}
\le c_k\Lambda^{e_k},
\tag{2.5}
\]

uniformly in \(m\). This proves the horizontal-projector part of BW-SIZE-FIXED-MARGIN.

## 3. Horizontal lift and quotient connection

For \(A=LL^T\) and \(U\in{\rm Sym}(m)\), let

\[
S_U=\mathcal L_A^{-1}U,
\qquad
\mathscr H(L,U)=S_UL.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then \(\mathscr H(L,U)\in\mathcal H_L\) and
\(d\pi_L\mathscr H(L,U)=U\). Differentiating the Sylvester equation

\[
AS_U+S_UA=U
\tag{3.2}
\]

has the same recurrence as (2.4). Hence

\[
\|D^k\mathscr H(L,U)\|_{F^{k+1}\to F}
\le c_k\Lambda^{e_k},
\qquad k\le k_0.
\tag{3.3}
\]

For constant-coordinate base fields \(U,V\), the quotient connection has horizontal lift

\[
\overline{\nabla_UV}(L)
=P_L^{\mathcal H}
D_L\{\mathscr H(L,V)\}[\mathscr H(L,U)].
\tag{3.4}
\]

For nonconstant \(V(A)\), add
\(\mathscr H\{L,DV[A]U\}\). Equations (2.5) and (3.3), followed by the finite-product rule, give

\[
\|D^k\Gamma^{\rm BW}\|_{F^{k+2}\to F}
\le c_k\Lambda^{e_k},
\qquad k\le k_0-1.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus the quotient connection and every connection derivative consumed by connection variation are dimension-uniform on the fixed margins.

## 4. O'Neill curvature

The total space \({\rm GL}(m)\) has the flat Frobenius connection. Its O'Neill tensor is

\[
\mathcal A_XY
=P_L^{\mathcal V}D\bar Y[L]\bar X,
\qquad
P_L^{\mathcal V}=I-P_L^{\mathcal H},
\tag{4.1}
\]

for basic horizontal lifts. The horizontal lift of the quotient curvature is a universal finite signed sum of terms of the forms

\[
\mathcal A_X\mathcal A_YZ,\qquad
\mathcal A_Y\mathcal A_XZ,\qquad
\mathcal A_{[X,Y]^{\mathcal H}}Z,
\tag{4.2}
\]

equivalently the standard O'Neill curvature formula for a flat total space. There is no trace or coordinate sum in (4.1)–(4.2). Equations (2.5) and (3.3) yield

\[
\|R^{\rm BW}(U,V)W\|_{\rm BW}
\le C_R\|U\|_{\rm BW}\|V\|_{\rm BW}\|W\|_{\rm BW},
\tag{4.3}
\]

\[
\|D^kR^{\rm BW}\|
\le c_k\Lambda^{e_k},
\qquad k\le k_0-2,
\tag{4.4}
\]

with constants independent of \(m\). This closes the curvature and connection-variation consumer.

## 5. Parallel transport and its parameter derivatives

Let \(L(t)\) be a horizontal lifted path and \(H(t)\in\mathcal H_{L(t)}\). Parallel transport satisfies

\[
P_{L(t)}^{\mathcal H}\dot H(t)=0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Thus \(\dot H=L\Omega\) for a skew \(\Omega\). Differentiating
\(L^TH=H^TL\) gives the explicit ODE

\[
\mathscr S_{L^TL}(\Omega)
=H^T\dot L-\dot L^TH,
\tag{5.2}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\dot H
=L\mathscr S_{L^TL}^{-1}
(H^T\dot L-\dot L^TH).}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The coefficient in this linear ODE obeys

\[
\|\dot H\|_F
\le{\beta\over\alpha}\|\dot L\|_{\rm op}\|H\|_F.
\tag{5.4}
\]

On a radial or polygonal segment parameterised on \([0,1]\),
\(\|\dot L\|_{\rm op}\le\|\dot L\|_F\le r_0\).
Gronwall therefore gives

\[
\|H(t)\|_F
\le\exp\{(\beta/\alpha)r_0\}\|H(0)\|_F.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Parallel transport is actually isometric in BW norm; (5.5) is the ambient bound used for parameter derivatives.

Let \(\theta\) denote any endpoint or ruled-surface parameter. Differentiating (5.3) gives

\[
\partial_t(\partial_\theta^\nu H)
=B(L,\dot L)\partial_\theta^\nu H+F_\nu,
\tag{5.6}
\]

where \(F_\nu\) is a universal finite sum of lower derivatives of \(H,L,\dot L\) and derivatives of the Sylvester inverse already controlled in Section 2. Induction, (5.5), and variation of constants yield

\[
\|D_\theta^k{\rm PT}\|
\le
c_k\Lambda^{e_k}
\exp\{c_kr_0\Lambda^{e_k}\},
\qquad k\le k_0.
\tag{5.7}
\]

The same proof applies to radial, polygonal, and two-parameter ruled-family transport. Corners introduce composition, not a singular derivative.

## 6. Exp, Log, observation Hessian, and normal radius

At principal lifts \(L=A^{1/2}\), \(M=B^{1/2}\), let

\[
N=M\operatorname{polar}(M^TL),\qquad H=N-L.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then

\[
\Log_AB=d\pi_L(H),
\qquad
\Exp_A\{d\pi_L(H)\}=\pi(L+H).
\tag{6.2}
\]

On the fixed spectral, polar, and Exp margins, (1.5), the product rule, and the lift/projector estimates give

\[
\max_{j\le k_0}
\{\|D^j\Exp\|,\|D^j\Log\|\}
\le c_{k_0}\Lambda^{e_{k_0}}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

The observation Hessian is

\[
H(A,B)=-\nabla_A\Log_AB.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Combining (3.5) and (6.3) gives

\[
\max_{j\le k_0-1}\|D^jH(A,B)\|
\le C_H(\alpha,\beta,\chi,k_0)
\le C_{\rm BW}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

At \(A=B\), \(H(A,A)=I\). Along the BW geodesic from \(A\) to \(B\), base-varying tangent spaces are identified by the dimension-uniform parallel transport from Section 5. Therefore

\[
\|H(A,B)-I\|_{\rm op}
\le C_H\,d_{\rm BW}(A,B).
\tag{6.6}
\]

If \(d_{\rm BW}(A,B)\le r_{\rm nor}\) from (0.4), then (0.5) follows. This supplies a dimension-uniform positive-Hessian and normal-radius theorem rather than assuming fixed-\(m\) compactness.

## 7. Richardson, blend, chord, and ruled-surface maps

\[
\|D^k\mathscr R\|\le c_k\Lambda^{e_k},
\qquad k\le k_0.
\tag{7.2}

The fixed-width blend and geodesic chord are the same composition with one scalar interpolation parameter. A ruled comparison surface is obtained from two such geodesic families and the parameter-dependent transport equation (5.3). Equations (4.4) and (5.7) therefore give

\[
\max_{j\le k_0}
\{\|D^j{\rm Blend}\|,\|D^j{\rm Chord}\|,

\tag{7.3}

No estimate uses the Frobenius norm of a base lift; only fixed-radius lift differences and operator norms occur.
