# P1-ID-B — curved reference change and rigidity

## 0. Adjudicated scope

The unique pointwise Fréchet mean of a known marginal law is already a law-functional. Nothing below reopens that elementary result. This dossier concerns only a *reference* \(c\) which is not required to be that mean.

The proposed generic rigidity statement is false. Exact observation-law equivalence identifies the orbit of admissible reference representations under compatible normal-chart transitions, not a preferred reference. Within that orbit, even the minimum dynamic rank can change. Exact rank preservation has a sharp support-level boundary: preservation of every finite subset's affine-hull dimension by the chart transition and its inverse. This is a rank-preservation property, not a claim that the chart transition is an ordinary affine map on the whole support. A common injective geodesic and a common simply connected totally geodesic flat satisfy the property because their transitions are affine; an analytic \(S^2\) example violates it and inflates rank one to rank two.

No Taylor remainder is used to prove equality of laws or finite-displacement rigidity.

## 1. Exact domains, typing, connectors, and first variation

### Definition B1 (common normal branch)

Fix \(x,y\in M\). Choose open sets \(V_x\subset T_xM\), \(V_y\subset T_yM\) such that

\[
 \operatorname{Exp}_x:V_x\longrightarrow N_x,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Exp}_y:V_y\longrightarrow N_y
\]

are diffeomorphisms onto open normal neighbourhoods. Choose an open observation domain

\[
 U\subset N_x\cap N_y,
 \qquad
 D_{x\to y}:=\operatorname{Exp}_x^{-1}(U)\subset T_xM.
\]

The exact base-point change is

\[
 \Phi_{x\to y}:D_{x\to y}\longrightarrow \log_y(U)\subset T_yM,
 \qquad
 \Phi_{x\to y}(v)=\log_y(\operatorname{Exp}_x v).
 \tag{B1.1}
\]

It is a smooth diffeomorphism, with inverse \(\Phi_{y\to x}\) on the corresponding branch. If \(X_t\in U\) almost surely, then pointwise, not merely in distribution,

\[
 Y_t^y=\Phi_{x\to y}(Y_t^x),
 \qquad Y_t^x=\log_xX_t.
 \tag{B1.2}
\]

If \(x\) and \(y\) are joined by a declared unique geodesic inside the branch, let \(P_{x\to y}:T_xM\to T_yM\) be Levi--Civita parallel transport along it. The connector-identified map in one fibre is

\[
 \bar\Phi_{x\to y}:=P_{y\to x}\Phi_{x\to y}:D_{x\to y}\to T_xM.
 \tag{B1.3}
\]

Thus \(\Phi(v)-v\) is ill-typed; \(\bar\Phi(v)-v\) is typed. The connector is only a comparison isometry. In general it does not linearise \(\Phi\).

### Lemma B1.1 (exact first variation)

For \(p=\operatorname{Exp}_xv\in U\),

\[
 D\Phi_{x\to y}(v)
 =D(\log_y)_p\circ D(\operatorname{Exp}_x)_v:T_xM\to T_yM.
 \tag{B1.4}
\]

Both factors and hence \(D\Phi(v)\) are invertible on the declared branches. If \(h\in T_xM\), \(D\operatorname{Exp}_x(v)h=J_h(1)\), where \(J_h\) is the Jacobi field along \(\tau\mapsto\operatorname{Exp}_x(\tau v)\) generated by the variation \(v+sh\), with \(J_h(0)=0\) and \(\nabla_\tau J_h(0)=h\). The differential of \(\log_y\) is the inverse endpoint Jacobi map on the \(y\)-normal branch.

**Proof.** Equation (B1.4) is the chain rule between the displayed tangent fibres. The normal-branch hypotheses make Exp and Log inverse diffeomorphisms. The Jacobi description is the definition of the variation field of a geodesic variation. \(\square\)

Higher variations are **SUPERSEDED/BYPASSED**. No theorem below consumes an infinitesimal approximation: the orbit identities, the flat reductions, the reset-chain rank classification, and the curved counterexample are all finite-displacement equalities. Consequently a second- or third-order expansion could not strengthen any load-bearing conclusion here.

### Lemma B1.2 (cut-locus boundary is genuine)

Without a common normal branch, (B1.1) need not be a function. On the unit sphere, if \(p=-y\), there are infinitely many minimizing geodesics from \(y\) to \(p\), so \(\log_yp\) is multivalued. Hence no global single-valued \(\Phi_{x\to y}\), path-law pushforward, or affine representation in \(T_yM\) exists without a branch or selection convention.

**Status:** **PROVED INTERNALLY.** This is a topology/cut-locus failure, independent of factor identification.

## 2. What is identified: the exact compatible-chart orbit

Let \({\cal P}\) be a path law whose coordinates lie in the declared common domains. Write

\[
 {\cal L}_x:=((\log_x)^{\mathbb Z})_\#{\cal P}
\]

for its tangent-coordinate path law at \(x\). Let \({\cal C}_x\) be any explicitly declared tangent model class at \(x\), for example rank-\(r\) affine factor plus temporally white noise with two-sided factor--noise lag orthogonality.

### Theorem B2 (exact identified orbit; generic reference rigidity is disproved)

For two references, use the common observation domain \(U_{xy}\) and tangent domain \(D_{x\to y}\) of Definition B1. For composition through three references, fix an open triple overlap
\[
 U_{xyz}\subset N_x\cap N_y\cap N_z
\]
on which all three selected Exp/Log branches are inverse, and restrict the source to
\[
 D_x^{xyz}:=\log_x(U_{xyz}).
\]
Then:

1. \(\Phi_{x\to x}=\mathrm{id}\), \(\Phi_{y\to x}=\Phi_{x\to y}^{-1}\), and
   \[
   \Phi_{y\to z}\circ\Phi_{x\to y}=\Phi_{x\to z}
   \quad\text{on }D_x^{xyz}.
   \tag{B2.1}
   \]
2. Coordinate path laws obey
   \[
   {\cal L}_y=((\Phi_{x\to y})^{\mathbb Z})_\#{\cal L}_x.
   \tag{B2.2}
   \]
3. Conversely, (B2.2) implies that exponentiating either coordinate law gives the same observation path law. Therefore the complete observational-equivalence class under weakened centring is exactly the orbit under these pairwise transitions and their compositions on explicitly declared multiple overlaps, restricted to
   \[
   \operatorname{Adm}({\cal P})
   :=\{x:{\cal L}_x\in{\cal C}_x\}.
   \tag{B2.3}
   \]
   Internal factor gauges and factor/noise reallocations are then quotiented within each fibre.
4. There is no universal uniqueness theorem on the weakened-reference class: although \(\operatorname{Adm}({\cal P})\) can be a singleton for a particular restricted model, a non-degenerate persistent process supported on one sufficiently short injective geodesic in a curved manifold has a continuum of admissible references on that geodesic by Theorem B3 below.

**Proof.** For \(v\in D_x^{xyz}\) and \(p=\operatorname{Exp}_xv\in U_{xyz}\),

\[
 \Phi_{y\to z}(\Phi_{x\to y}(v))
 =\log_z\!\left(\operatorname{Exp}_y(\log_yp)\right)
 =\log_zp
 =\Phi_{x\to z}(v).
\]

The identity and inverse statements are the special cases \(z=x\) and \(y=x\). Applying this equality at every time proves (B2.2). Conversely, \(X_t=\operatorname{Exp}_xY_t^x=\operatorname{Exp}_yY_t^y\) pointwise, so all finite-dimensional observation laws agree. The final assertion follows from the exact translation in Theorem B3 and the persistent reset-chain construction in Theorem B5. \(\square\)

This is not a relabelling of the conclusion as an assumption: (B2.1)--(B2.2) prove the exact equivalence relation from Exp/Log inversion, while (B2.3) records which orbit members satisfy the independently specified probabilistic model class.

**Status:** generic uniqueness **DISPROVED**; exact orbit **SHARPLY REFORMULATED AND PROVED**.

## 3. Exact common-geodesic and flat reductions

### Theorem B3 (common injective geodesic)

Let \(\gamma:I\to M\) be unit-speed and injective on an interval containing \(a,b\) and the support parameters. Assume the relevant subarc lies in the common normal branch, set \(x=\gamma(a)\), \(y=\gamma(b)\), \(u_x=\dot\gamma(a)\), \(u_y=\dot\gamma(b)\), and let \(P=P_{x\to y}\) along \(\gamma\). For

\[
 v_s=(s-a)u_x,
 \qquad w=\log_xy=(b-a)u_x,
\]

one has the exact identity

\[
 \Phi_{x\to y}(v_s)=(s-b)u_y=P(v_s-w).
 \tag{B3.1}
\]

Consequently, if an affine factor representation is supported in that line,

\[
 Y_t^x=m+Af_t+\delta_t,
\]

then

\[
 Y_t^y=m'+A'f_t+\delta_t',\qquad
 m'=P(m-w),\quad A'=PA,\quad\delta_t'=P\delta_t.
 \tag{B3.2}
\]

Every condition expressed through equality of finite-dimensional laws, independence, temporal whiteness, two-sided factor--noise lag orthogonality, covariance rank, or Hilbert norm is preserved because \(P\) is a deterministic linear isometry. A zero-intercept constraint at both references is not preserved unless \(m=w\); that is a centring convention, not observation-law identification.

**Proof.** The geodesic identity \(\operatorname{Exp}_x((s-a)u_x)=\gamma(s)\) and uniqueness of the selected Log give \(\log_y\gamma(s)=(s-b)u_y\). Parallel transport preserves the tangent of a geodesic, \(Pu_x=u_y\), proving (B3.1). Linearity and isometry of \(P\) prove (B3.2) and every stated preservation claim. \(\square\)

**Status:** **PROVED INTERNALLY.** Ambient curvature is irrelevant because the support sees only one affine geodesic coordinate.

### Theorem B4 (common simply connected totally geodesic flat)

Let \(F\subset M\) be a complete simply connected totally geodesic flat. Assume \(x,y\in F\), the observation support lies in a subset \(U\subset F\) on which intrinsic and ambient Exp/Log are the same unique branches, and \(P=P_{x\to y}\) is transport in \(F\). Then for every \(v\in T_xF\) with \(\operatorname{Exp}_xv\in U\),

\[
 \Phi_{x\to y}(v)=P(v-w),\qquad w=\log_xy.
 \tag{B4.1}
\]

Thus every affine factor-plus-white-noise representation supported in \(T_xF\) transforms exactly as in (B3.2), in any rank.

**Proof.** A complete simply connected flat is isometric, by its developing coordinates, to a Euclidean space. Total geodesy makes its intrinsic geodesics and parallel transport agree with the ambient ones on the declared unique branch. In the Euclidean development based at \(x\), the point \(p=\operatorname{Exp}_xv\) has coordinate \(v\), \(y\) has coordinate \(w\), and the vector from \(y\) to \(p\), transported back to \(x\), is \(v-w\). Transporting to \(T_yF\) gives (B4.1). The representation claim follows exactly as in Theorem B3. \(\square\)

The assumptions are not cosmetic. Simple connectivity removes flat holonomy and multivalued global developments; the common ambient branch removes competing ambient logs; total geodesy keeps Exp, Log, and transport inside \(F\); zero curvature is what makes the development affine. The sharp support-level condition, however, is given next and can also hold on a single geodesic inside a curved manifold.

**Status:** **PROVED INTERNALLY.**

## 4. Sharp exact support boundary via persistent Markov probes

For a second-order stationary Hilbert process \(Z_t\), define its included-lag dynamic span

\[
 {\cal D}(Z)=\overline{\operatorname{span}}
 \{\operatorname{ran}\Gamma_Z(h):h\ge1\},
 \qquad
 \Gamma_Z(h)=E[(Z_t-EZ_t)\otimes(Z_{t-h}-EZ_t)].
 \tag{B5.1}
\]

Under an affine rank-\(r\) factor representation with temporally white idiosyncratic noise and both factor--noise cross-lag directions zero, \({\cal D}(Z)\subseteq\operatorname{ran}A\), hence \(\dim{\cal D}(Z)\le r\).

### Lemma B5.1 (reset-chain rank identity)

Let \(S_t\) be stationary on a finite set \(\{1,\ldots,k\}\) with strictly positive invariant probabilities \(\pi_i\) and transition

\[
 \Pr(S_t=j\mid S_{t-1}=i)=\rho\,1_{\{i=j\}}+(1-\rho)\pi_j,
 \qquad 0<\rho<1.
 \tag{B5.2}
\]

For any map \(g:\{1,\ldots,k\}\to H\),

\[
 \Gamma_{g(S)}(h)=\rho^h\Sigma_g,
 \qquad
 \Sigma_g=E[(g(S)-\bar g)\otimes(g(S)-\bar g)],\quad h\ge1,
 \tag{B5.3}
\]

and

\[
 \operatorname{ran}\Sigma_g
 =\operatorname{span}\{g(i)-\bar g:1\le i\le k\}.
 \tag{B5.4}
\]

Therefore its minimum dynamic rank is exactly the affine-hull dimension of \(\{g(i)\}\).

**Proof.** The transition operator is the identity times \(\rho\) plus projection onto constants times \(1-\rho\). Hence every mean-zero \(H\)-valued function obeys

\[
 E[g(S_t)-\bar g\mid S_{t-h}]=\rho^h(g(S_{t-h})-\bar g),
\]

which gives (B5.3). For \(q\in H\),

\[
 \langle\Sigma_gq,q\rangle
 =\sum_i\pi_i\langle g(i)-\bar g,q\rangle^2.
\]

Since every \(\pi_i>0\), its nullspace is the orthogonal complement of the span in (B5.4). \(\square\)

### Theorem B5 (necessary-and-sufficient finite-subset rank classification)

Let \(E\subset D_{x\to y}\) and let \(r=\operatorname{affdim}E<\infty\). For every finite \(V\subset E\), put a reset chain with positive mass on each point of \(V\). Then the minimum dynamic ranks at the two references are exactly

\[
 r_x(V)=\operatorname{affdim}V,
 \qquad
 r_y(V)=\operatorname{affdim}\Phi_{x\to y}(V).
 \tag{B5.5}
\]

In particular, the base change preserves the bound \(r_y(V)\le r\) for every such persistent probe if and only if

\[
 \operatorname{affdim}\Phi_{x\to y}(E)\le r.
 \tag{B5.6}
\]

It preserves exact rank in both directions on all finite probes precisely when every finite \(V\subset E\) and every finite \(W\subset\Phi(E)\) obey
\[
 \operatorname{affdim}\Phi(V)=\operatorname{affdim}V,\qquad
 \operatorname{affdim}\Phi^{-1}(W)=\operatorname{affdim}W.
 \tag{B5.7}
\]
This finite-configuration condition does not imply that \(\Phi|_E\) is an affine map: for example, \(t\mapsto t^3\) on an interval of \(\mathbb R\) preserves affine dimension of every finite subset but is nonlinear. Common geodesics and common flats satisfy (B5.7) for the stronger reason that (B3.1) and (B4.1) are affine. If (B5.6) fails, a finite subset of at most \(r+2\) points already witnesses the failure and gives an exact persistent factor law whose minimum dynamic rank increases.

**Proof.** Apply Lemma B5.1 first to the inclusion \(g_x(v)=v\), then to \(g_y(v)=\Phi(v)\), proving (B5.5). If (B5.6) holds, every finite image has affine dimension at most \(r\). Conversely, if the affine hull of \(\Phi(E)\) has dimension at least \(r+1\), by the definition of affine dimension it contains \(r+2\) affinely independent image points; their preimages form the finite witness. Applying the same argument to \(\Phi^{-1}\) gives the two-sided assertion. \(\square\)

This is the promised genuine mathematical boundary. Ambient flatness and exact affine transitions are sufficient but not necessary; (B5.7), or the one-sided bound (B5.6) when only non-inflation is required, is necessary and sufficient for universal rank preservation on the declared finite reset-probe class. The probe class has bounded support, geometric mixing, a nonzero ordinary persistence parameter \(0<\rho<1\), no deterministic trend, and no zero-frequency atom.

**Status:** **PROVED INTERNALLY.**

## 5. Exact analytic curved counterexample

### Theorem B6 (curvature-induced rank inflation on the unit sphere)

Let \(M=S^2\subset\mathbb R^3\) with its unit-round metric. Put

\[
 x=(0,0,1),\quad e_1=(1,0,0),\quad e_2=(0,1,0),\quad
 y=(1,0,0).
\]

At \(y\), use the orthonormal basis

\[
 E_1=(0,0,-1),\qquad E_2=(0,1,0).
\]

Choose

\[
 0<b<\frac{\pi}{2(1+\sqrt2)},\qquad 0<\rho<1,
\]

and let \(F_t\in\{-b,0,b\}\) be the stationary uniform reset chain (B5.2). Define

\[
 Y_t^x=F_te_2,
 \qquad X_t=\operatorname{Exp}_x(Y_t^x),
 \qquad A:\mathbb R\to T_xS^2,\quad Aa=ae_2,
 \qquad \delta_t=0.
 \tag{B6.1}
\]

Then all of the following hold.

1. **Normal domains.** Since \(|F_t|\le b<\pi\), \(\log_xX_t=F_te_2\) is unique. Moreover
   \[
   X_t=(0,\sin F_t,\cos F_t),\qquad y\cdot X_t=0,
   \]
   so \(d(y,X_t)=\pi/2\) and \(X_t\ne-y\); \(\log_yX_t\) is unique.
2. **Mean.** The marginal Fréchet mean exists and is uniquely \(x\), although neither representation below needs to declare its reference a mean. Indeed
   \[
   F_Q(x)=\frac{2b^2}{3}.
   \]
   Any possible competitor \(q\) with \(d(q,x)>\sqrt2b\) has
   \(F_Q(q)\ge d(q,x)^2/3>F_Q(x)\), because the law gives mass \(1/3\) to \(x\). At \(d(q,x)=\sqrt2b\) the same lower bound is an equality only for the contribution of the atom at \(x\), while the other two nonnegative contributions are not both zero, so again \(F_Q(q)>F_Q(x)\). It remains to consider the cap \(d(q,x)<\sqrt2b<\pi/2\), which is geodesically convex: the shorter great-circle interpolation is a normalized positive linear combination of its endpoints, and its scalar product with \(x\) remains at least \(\cos(\sqrt2b)\). Every distance from this cap to a support point is below \((1+\sqrt2)b<\pi/2\). In round-sphere polar coordinates about such a support point, \(g=dr^2+\sin^2r\,g_{S^1}\), so \(\operatorname{Hess}r=\cot r\,(g-dr\otimes dr)\) and
   \[
   \operatorname{Hess}\!\left(\tfrac12r^2\right)
   =dr\otimes dr+r\cot r\,(g-dr\otimes dr).
   \]
   Its radial eigenvalue is \(1\) and its transverse eigenvalue is \(r\cot r>0\). Thus \(F_Q\) is strictly geodesically convex on the only cap that can contain a minimizer. Symmetry gives
   \(\sum_p\log_xp=0\), so \(x\) is its sole critical point and unique global minimizer.
3. **Rank-one persistent representation at \(x\).** \(EF_t=0\),
   \[
   \operatorname{Cov}(F_t,F_{t-h})=\rho^h\frac{2b^2}{3},\qquad h\ge1.
   \]
   Thus (B6.1) is an exact rank-one persistent affine factor model. The zero noise is independent, temporally white, and two-sided cross-lag orthogonal.
4. **Exact nonlinear chart transition.** For every \(t\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\),
   \[
   \Phi_{x\to y}(te_2)
   =\frac\pi2\{-\cos(t)E_1+\sin(t)E_2\}.
   \tag{B6.2}
   \]
   This follows from the exact spherical formula
   \(\log_yp=\theta[p-(y\cdot p)y]/\sin\theta\) with
   \(\theta=\arccos(y\cdot p)=\pi/2\).
5. **Rank inflation.** The three values in (B6.2) at \(-b,0,b\) are not collinear: the first coordinate at \(0\) is \(-\pi/2\), while at \(\pm b\) it is \(-\tfrac\pi2\cos b\), and the second coordinates have opposite nonzero signs. Let \(G_t=\Phi(F_te_2)\) and \(k=\pi/2\). Its stationary covariance in the \(E_1,E_2\) basis is diagonal with positive entries
   \[
   \Sigma_G=
   \begin{pmatrix}
   \frac{2k^2}{9}(1-\cos b)^2&0\\[2mm]
   0&\frac{2k^2}{3}\sin^2b
   \end{pmatrix},
   \qquad
   \Gamma_G(h)=\rho^h\Sigma_G.
   \tag{B6.3}
   \]
   Hence the minimum dynamic rank at \(y\) is two.
6. **Violation of fixed-rank preservation.** If \(G_t=m'+a'f_t'+\delta_t'\) had scalar factor, temporally white \(\delta'\), and both factor--noise cross-lag directions zero, every nonzero-lag covariance would have range in \(\operatorname{span}\{a'\}\) and rank at most one, contradicting (B6.3). It does have an exact rank-two representation with loading \(I_{T_yS^2}\), factor \(G_t-EG_t\), and zero noise.

Thus the same bounded, geometrically mixing observation process has an exact rank-one affine representation at one weakened reference and exact minimum dynamic rank two at another. The failure is curvature-specific in this construction, not a cut-locus, zero-frequency, nonunique-mean, deterministic-trend, or coloured-noise artifact. Formula (B6.2) is the non-affine circular arc responsible for it.

**Status:** generic fixed-rank reference invariance **DISPROVED**; the finite-subset rank-preservation repair in Theorem B5 is sharp.

## 6. Factor-plus-white-noise adjudication

Fix the primitive class in which

\[
 Y_t=m+Af_t+\delta_t,\qquad A:\mathbb R^r\to H\text{ injective},
\]

\(\delta\) is temporally uncorrelated at every nonzero lag, and both

\[
 E[(Af_t-EAf_t)\otimes\delta_{t-h}]=0,\qquad
 E[\delta_t\otimes(Af_{t-h}-EAf_{t-h})]=0
\]

hold for every nonzero lag. Then:

- on a common geodesic or common flat, Theorems B3--B4 preserve the complete representation exactly, including independence if it was assumed;
- on a general normal domain, the observation law always transforms exactly by Theorem B2, but membership in the same fixed-rank affine/noise class is not invariant;
- Theorem B5 gives a necessary-and-sufficient exact classification on the zero-noise persistent probe subclass, which is already sufficient to disprove every proposed universal preservation theorem for the larger factor-plus-white-noise class;
- Theorem B6 shows that adding a nondegeneracy requirement to persistence, unique Fréchet means, bounded support, mixing, or normal-domain control does not repair the theorem. The load-bearing repair for the reset-probe class is the exact finite-subset affine-dimension condition (or an explicit convention fixing the reference), not generic regularity.

No claim is made that every special law on a curved support must suffer rank inflation. Radial/common-geodesic laws do not. The exact identified target is the orbit, and minimum rank is fibre/reference dependent unless the finite-subset rank-preservation property is imposed for the law class being compared.
