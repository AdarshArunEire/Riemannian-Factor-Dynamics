### 1.3 Bucci, Palma & Zhang (2024/2025), arXiv:2412.09517 — full-text findings

Full text obtained (all sections, including equations and both result tables) via the alphaXiv PDF-query tool, cross-checked against the arXiv HTML rendering. Quotes below are verbatim from the extracted text; equation/section/table numbers are the paper's own.

**Training loss (Section 3, esp. §3.2 "Geometric HAR (GeoHAR)").**
The paper's own novel geometric loss is the **log-Euclidean distance**, defined at Eq. (7):
> "d(S₁, S₂) = ‖logm(S₁) − logm(S₂)‖_F" — Eq. (7), §3.2.

This is used both (i) to compute the Fréchet sample mean that builds the GeoHAR input (Eq. 8) and (ii) directly **as the network's training loss function**. Verbatim, §4.3: "the ReSPDNET models are trained either using the mean squared error or log-Euclidean loss functions," and the paper's stated headline finding (Conclusions, §6): "a model with ten lags and trained using the logarithmic Euclidean distance as a loss function … perform[s] best. … using an Euclidean loss function to train a neural network for SPD matrices leads to poor predictive results."
→ **Confirmed**: the paper's preferred/best-performing training loss is a Riemannian **geodesic distance on the SPD manifold** (log-Euclidean), used in explicit contrast to a Euclidean/MSE loss. This matches the "Riemannian (SPD-manifold geodesic) loss" element of the claim under audit.

**Forecast target.**
The target is simply the next-day realized covariance matrix itself: the dataset is built as pairs {(D_t, Y_t)} with Y_t = RC_t (§3.1), RC_t defined at Eq. (1) as the intraday sum of squared/cross-product returns. The paper does **not** frame its target in terms of "the conditional expectation of the latent/integrated covariance" or any other explicit functional-of-the-conditional-law language; it simply predicts next-period RC_t. This is narrower and less explicit than the campaign's locked target-functional vocabulary (§2 of the lead ledger) — the paper is silent on whether RC_t is being treated as an unbiased proxy for a conditional-mean target or as the target in its own right.

**Evaluation metrics (Section 4.3, Table 2/3; Section 5, Table 4).**
Statistical accuracy: Frobenius, Euclidean and Procrustes distances, "used as both metrics and loss functions" (§4.3), fed into a Model Confidence Set test (Hansen, Lunde & Nason 2011). Economic/portfolio evaluation: **Global Minimum Variance (GMV) and long-only GMV⁺ portfolios** (Section 5), with weights from
> "arg min w'_{t+1} R̂C_{t+1} w_{t+1} s.t. w'_{t+1}ι = 1" (§5, unnumbered display eq.),
reported via annualized portfolio standard deviation σ_p and turnover τ_p (Table 4). → **Confirmed**: the paper's economic-value evaluation is exactly a GMV-portfolio evaluation, matching the claim's "global-minimum-variance (GMV) portfolio evaluation" element.

**Discussion of proxy noise / conditional unbiasedness / loss robustness to an imperfect proxy.**
The paper does contain one, and only one, sentence on proxy-robustness of a loss/metric, and it is scoped narrowly:
> "According to Laurent et al. (2013), the first two distances [Frobenius, Euclidean] demonstrate robustness in relation to volatility proxies." (§4.3, p.14, citing Laurent, Rombouts & Violante (2013), *Journal of Econometrics* 173(1):1–10 — the same paper this campaign is independently verifying under LO-1/L-1.6).

This sentence attributes proxy-robustness **only to the Frobenius and Euclidean distances used as statistical evaluation metrics**, not to the log-Euclidean distance that is the paper's actual novel training loss (and best-performing model), not to the Procrustes distance, and not to the GMV portfolio evaluation of Section 5. Beyond this one citation, exhaustive search of the extracted full text (search for "loss," "microstructure," "noise," "unbiased," "proxy," "robust," across all sections) turned up **no other occurrence**. The paper never discusses: (i) whether RC_t (its target/proxy) is conditionally unbiased for any latent integrated-covariance functional; (ii) whether its log-Euclidean training loss is robust to proxy noise in the sense of Patton (2011) / Laurent–Rombouts–Violante (2013); (iii) whether the GMV portfolio evaluation in Section 5 can or cannot detect a ranking distortion induced by training on a non-robust loss. These are not addressed anywhere in the paper — this is recorded as **silence**, not as an error by the authors.

### 1.4 LO-7 verdict — narrowest claim the evidence supports

**Verdict: candidate identified and verified verbatim; claim upheld only in its structural (loss + evaluation-instrument) part, and explicitly NOT in its bias-detection-failure part, which the paper does not address.**

Precisely:
1. Bucci, Palma & Zhang, "Geometric Deep Learning for Realized Covariance Matrix Forecasting" (arXiv:2412.09517, posted 2024-12-12, still an unpublished working paper as of the author's own site — **not** confirmed as journal-published; see §1.5 caveat) is a geometric-deep-learning covariance-forecasting paper whose best-performing, headline training loss is a Riemannian/SPD-manifold geodesic loss (log-Euclidean distance, Eq. 7), and which evaluates forecasts economically via a Global Minimum Variance portfolio (Section 5, Table 4). 2. ## Job 2 — measurement-error literature check: unbiased vs. consistent

### 2(a) Continuous Itô semimartingale, no microstructure noise: is realized covariance conditionally unbiased for integrated covariance?

Primary source: **Barndorff-Nielsen & Shephard (2002), "Estimating quadratic variation using realized variance," *Journal of Applied Econometrics* 17(5):457–477** (scalar case; the matrix/multivariate generalization is in Barndorff-Nielsen & Shephard (2004), *Econometrica* 72(3):885–925, "Econometric Analysis of Realized Covariation").

Full text of the 2002 JAE paper was read (via WebFetch on the author's posted copy). The paper decomposes the observed process y as y = α (drift) + m (local martingale / diffusion part), and derives the exact conditional expectation of realized variance (quoted verbatim, their notation):
> "E{[y_M]₁ | F₀} = E{[α_M]₁ | F₀} + 2E{[α_M, m_M]₁ | F₀} + E{[m]₁ | F₀}"

i.e. the conditional expectation of realized variance = (drift quadratic-variation term) + (2 × drift–martingale cross-covariation term) + (quadratic variation of the martingale part = integrated variance). They then state the exact unbiasedness case:
> "The simplest such condition is when α is zero. In this case E{[y^Ł]_h̄ | F₀} = E{[y^Ł_M]₁ | F₀} exactly, by the properties of local martingales."

So: **conditional unbiasedness of realized variance for integrated variance holds exactly only when the drift α is identically zero** (or, more generally, when the drift-squared term and the drift–diffusion cross term both vanish in conditional expectation — the paper's conditions (16)–(17) give weaker sufficient conditions under which these terms vanish only asymptotically as the number of intraday intervals M → ∞, not for finite M). With a nonzero drift, the exact conditional expectation picks up the drift-squared term E{[α_M]₁|F₀} plus a drift–diffusion cross term 2E{[α_M,m_M]₁|F₀} — this is exactly the "drift-squared term" flagged in the campaign brief, verified verbatim. The 2002 paper's treatment is univariate/scalar; leverage (correlation between the driving Brownian motion of price and of volatility) is a property of m's own quadratic-variation limit theory (affects the *distribution*/CLT variance of the estimation error, via Barndorff-Nielsen & Shephard's later asymptotic-normality results) rather than the conditional-mean identity itself — the mean identity above holds regardless of leverage as long as α is zero; leverage affects only whether the *estimation-error* (not the conditional bias) is independent of price-volatility co-movement. This distinction (mean identity vs. asymptotic variance under leverage) is drawn in the paper's own asymptotic-normality section but is not re-verified verbatim here; flagged as **not independently re-checked** for the leverage-conditioning claim specifically — only the drift-zero unbiasedness identity was verified verbatim.

**Conclusion for (a):** realized (co)variance is exactly conditionally unbiased for integrated (co)variance under a continuous Itô semimartingale **only in the driftless case** (α ≡ 0); with a nonzero drift, the exact conditional expectation includes an extra drift-quadratic-variation term and a drift–diffusion cross term, verified verbatim in Barndorff-Nielsen & Shephard (2002), JAE, unnumbered display equation following their eq. system for E{[y_M]₁|F₀}. General (nonzero-drift, or leverage-conditioned) exact unbiasedness was **not found and is not claimed** by this source; only consistency (convergence in probability to QV) is established for the general driftful case.

### 2(b) Additive i.i.d. microstructure noise: naive RV biased upward by 2Mσ²_noise

Two independent primary sources confirm this, both read in full text:

- **Zhang, Mykland & Aït-Sahalia (2005), "A Tale of Two Time Scales: Determining Integrated Volatility with Noisy High-Frequency Data," *JASA* 100(472):1394–1411.** Verbatim (their eq. 18, as extracted): "E([Y,Y]^(all)_T | X process) = [X,X]^(all)_T + 2nE[ε²]" — i.e. the naive all-tick realized variance has conditional expectation equal to the true quadratic variation plus 2n·E[ε²], n being the number of intraday returns and E[ε²] the noise variance. **Confirmed verbatim, equation 18.**
- **Hansen & Lunde ("An Unbiased Measure of Realized Variance")**, Corollary 4 (as extracted): "bias(RV^(m)_[a,b]) = 2mπ(0)" where π(0) is the noise variance and m the number of intraday returns — the same 2Mσ²_noise formula in different notation. **Confirmed verbatim, Corollary 4.** (This paper additionally proposes an autocovariance-corrected estimator RV_AC that it proves exactly unbiased, its Theorem 5: "bias(RV^(m)_AC[a,b]) = 0" — noted for completeness; RV_AC is not one of the three noise-robust estimator families Job 2(c) asks about.)

**Conclusion for (b):** confirmed, with the standard references being Zhang, Mykland & Aït-Sahalia (2005) and Hansen & Lunde (Hansen, P.R. & Lunde, A., "An Unbiased Measure of Realized Variance," working paper, subsequently published as Hansen & Lunde (2006), *Journal of Business & Economic Statistics* 24(2):127–161 for the bias-correction/autocovariance estimator, though the bias-formula portion above was verified against the working-paper text specifically, not re-checked against the published JBES version).
