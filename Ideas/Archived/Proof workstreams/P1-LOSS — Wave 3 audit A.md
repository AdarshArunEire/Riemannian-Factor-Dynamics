# P1-LOSS — Wave 3 audit A

**Overall verdict.** The headline theorem **A-4.3 stands only with correction**. Its stated proof chain is broken at the first link: **A-1.1 is false as stated** (closed-form counterexample below) and its written proof contains a non sequitur that is independent of the falsity. The chain is repairable — I give the exact repaired statement and proof — but the repair *changes the hypotheses of the whole campaign*: it requires (a) the normalisation `L≥0, L(h,h)=0` to be moved **into** A-1.1, (b) continuity of `L(·,h)`, and (c) a **strictly stronger richness axiom** (three-point laws, not two-point laws — the two-point axiom is provably insufficient in dimension ≥ 2, i.e. for every matrix case the project cares about). Separately, A-4.3's statement is wrong in two smaller ways (`𝒜≻0` should be `𝒜⪰0`; "symmetric" does not typecheck on the stated domain `𝒴×ℋ`, and there is a counterexample exploiting exactly that). A-4.1, A-4.2, A-4.4, A-4.5, A-1.3, A-E4.1, A-E4.3, A-E4.4 survive. A-E4.2 and A-E1.2 are stated as "iff" and only one direction is proved; A-E4.2's "iff" is outright false as written.

## 2. The repair of A-1.1 (exact statement and proof)

The counterexample `L(y,h)=c(h)e^y` dies the instant the normalisation is imposed (`L(h,h)=c(h)e^h=0` forces `c≡0`), so the campaign's chain is recoverable — but only by **merging A-1.1 and A-1.2 into a single claim with the normalisation up front**, adding continuity, and strengthening richness. The following is a complete replacement for §2.1 that also makes the indifference-surface construction unnecessary.

> **A-1.1′.** Let `𝒴⊆V` be convex with `ℋ ⊆ 𝒴`, let `𝒬` contain every finitely supported law on `𝒴` with any given mean in `𝒴` (three atoms suffice), and let `L:𝒴×ℋ→[0,∞)` satisfy `L(h,h)=0`, `L(·,h)` continuous on `𝒴` and `Q`-integrable. If `L` is proxy-robust over `𝒬`, then for every `H,H₁∈ℋ` the difference `L(·,H)−L(·,H₁)` is affine on `𝒴`; hence `L(y,H)=ψ(y)+a(H)+⟨b(H),y⟩` with `ψ:=L(·,H₁)`.
>
> *Proof.* Fix `H,H₁∈ℋ` and put `Φ=L(·,H)−L(·,H₁)`. Then `Φ(H₁)=−L(H₁,H)≤0≤L(H,H₁)=Φ(H)`, and `[H₁,H]⊆𝒴`, so by continuity there is `Σ₀∈[H₁,H]` with `Φ(Σ₀)=0`. Robustness at mean `Σ₀` gives `sign E_QΦ = sign Φ(Σ₀)=0`, i.e. **`E_QΦ=0` for every `Q∈𝒬` with mean `Σ₀`**. Write `ρ(v)=Φ(Σ₀+v)`.
> *Homogeneity (two atoms).* For `r,s>0` and `v` with the atoms in `𝒴`, the law `(s δ_{Σ₀−rv} + r δ_{Σ₀+sv})/(r+s)` has mean `Σ₀`, so `sρ(−rv)+rρ(sv)=0`; taking `s=1` and `r=1` in turn gives `ρ(tv)=tρ(v)` for all `t∈ℝ`.
> *Additivity (three atoms).* The law `⅓(δ_{Σ₀+3u}+δ_{Σ₀+3w}+δ_{Σ₀−3(u+w)})` has mean `Σ₀`, so `ρ(3u)+ρ(3w)+ρ(−3(u+w))=0`, i.e. `ρ(u+w)=ρ(u)+ρ(w)`.
> Hence `ρ` is linear and `Φ` is affine near `Σ₀`; homogeneity then extends it to all of `𝒴` by convexity (`Φ(Σ₀+tv)=tρ(v)` for every admissible `t`). ∎

Notes on what this costs and buys. (i) Continuity is only used to produce **one** zero of `Φ`; if one is willing to assume `L(·,h)` continuous merely on the segment `[H₁,H]`, that suffices. (ii) With three-point richness the dimension-≥2 obstruction disappears **and no continuity is needed for the linearity step**, which is a strictly better trade than the dossier's current route. (iii) If one insists on keeping two-point richness, the argument can still be closed, but only with continuity and a longer route: the zero set `Z` of `Φ` is closed under lines (from homogeneity at each of its points), hence affine; `ρ` factors through `V/dir(Z)` (from `ρ(w+v)=ρ(sw+v)` for all `s>0`, letting `s↓0`); an odd continuous homogeneous function on a quotient of dimension ≥2 must vanish somewhere on the unit sphere by connectedness, contradicting `Z` being exactly that affine set; so the quotient is ≤1-dimensional and `ρ` is linear. Three-point laws are cheaper and are what the cited literature assumes anyway. (iv) After A-1.1′, §2.2 goes through **verbatim** with "`ℋ` open" replaced by "`H∈int 𝒴`", and A-4.3 follows with the two statement corrections listed in its row (`𝒜⪰0`; symmetry on `ℋ×ℋ` plus continuity on `𝒴`, or `𝒴=ℋ`).
