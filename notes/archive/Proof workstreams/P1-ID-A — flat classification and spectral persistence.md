# P1-ID-A — flat classification and spectral persistence

## 0. Scope, notation, and terminal claim register

This dossier distinguishes equality of marginal laws, equality of all finite-dimensional distributions (FDDs), equality of second-order lag objects, and recovery from one triangular-array path. It does not use one as a synonym for another.

Let $H$ be a real separable Hilbert space. For $x,y\in H$, $x\otimes y$ is the operator $v\mapsto\langle y,v\rangle x$. For a square-integrable weakly stationary process $Z$,
\[
\Gamma_Z(h)=\mathbb E[(Z_{t+h}-\mathbb EZ_0)\otimes(Z_t-\mathbb EZ_0)],
\qquad \Gamma_Z(-h)=\Gamma_Z(h)^*.
\]
All closed spans below are norm-closed; because the dynamic spaces used in the factor theorem are finite-dimensional, no topology ambiguity remains there.

## 1. Claim A0 — exact information-set relations

### Statement

1. Equality of all FDDs, $\mathcal I_J$-equivalence, implies equality of every one-time marginal law, $\mathcal I_M$-equivalence. The converse is false.
2. Any declared population lag row $(\mathbb E[(X_{t+h}-c)\otimes(X_t-c)])_{h\in\mathcal H}$ is a functional of the relevant two-time laws, hence of $\mathcal I_J$. Equality of that row does not imply equality of $\mathcal I_J$, even if all integer lags and the marginal law are included.
3. $\mathcal I_M$ and a nonzero-lag version of $\mathcal I_F(c)$ are incomparable: equal marginals can have different lag moments, and equal lag moments can have different marginals.
4. $\mathcal I_1$ is not an intermediate population information set. It is a sequence of statistical experiments generated by one triangular-array path. A population target first defined under $\mathcal I_M$ or $\mathcal I_J$ is recoverable under $\mathcal I_1$ only after a consistency theorem.
5. $\mathcal I_R$ is an equivalence relation on reference-based representations of a fixed observation law, generated by the exact coordinate map and internal factor gauges. It is not an implication in the information hierarchy.

### Proof

The first implication holds because one-dimensional distributions are among the FDDs. Strictness follows by taking a nondegenerate random variable $Z\sim Q$: the iid process $X_t\sim Q$ and the constant process $\widetilde X_t=Z$ have the same marginals and different two-time laws.

A lag moment is an integral against a two-time law, proving the second implication. To prove strictness even after fixing marginals and every autocovariance, let $(U_k,V_k)_{k\in\mathbb Z}$ be iid pairs of independent Rademacher variables. Form blocks $(U_k,V_k,U_kV_k)$, concatenate them, and independently choose a phase $J$ uniformly from $\{0,1,2\}$ to make the resulting bi-infinite process $Y_t$ stationary. A unit time shift cycles the phase, and a three-unit shift merely relabels iid blocks, so the phase mixture is strictly stationary.

Conditional on any phase, two distinct coordinates either lie in different independent blocks or form one of the within-block pairs $(U_k,V_k)$, $(U_k,U_kV_k)$, or $(V_k,U_kV_k)$. Each displayed pair is independent Rademacher: for example, the map $(U_k,V_k)\mapsto(U_k,U_kV_k)$ is a bijection of $\{-1,1\}^2$. Thus the conditional pair law is the same product-Rademacher law for every phase, and mixing over the phase preserves it. Hence every pair $Y_s,Y_t$, $s\ne t$, is independent Rademacher, every nonzero autocovariance is zero, and the marginals agree with an iid Rademacher process $R_t$. But
\[
\mathbb E[Y_tY_{t+1}Y_{t+2}]=\tfrac13,
\qquad
\mathbb E[R_tR_{t+1}R_{t+2}]=0.
\]
Indeed, exactly one of the three phases places the displayed triple inside a block, where its product is $U_kV_k(U_kV_k)=1$. In a second phase the product reduces to $U_kU_{k+1}$, whose expectation is zero; in the third it is $(U_kV_k)(U_{k+1}V_{k+1})$, also of expectation zero. Thus the FDDs differ although all second-order lags and marginals agree.

For incomparability, the iid and constant constructions have equal marginals but respectively zero and nonzero nonzero-lag covariance when $Q$ has positive variance. Conversely, centered iid $N(0,1)$ and iid Rademacher sequences have the same zero nonzero-lag row but different marginals. Items 4 and 5 follow from their definitions: an estimator and a representation orbit are not additional population laws. ∎

### Assumptions, consumer, weakness

This proof uses exact law equality, not asymptotic indistinguishability. It supplies ID-0 and prevents ID-2 or ID-5 from promoting a lag quotient to a full-law quotient. The phase-block construction should be retained in hostile review because it defeats the stronger “all autocovariances plus marginals determine the law” claim.

## 2. Claim A1 — independent audit of the unique-mean gate

### Statement

Let $(M,d)$ be a metric space and $Q\in\mathcal P_2(M)$ such that
\[
F_Q(x)=\int d(x,z)^2Q(dz)
\]
has a unique minimizer $m_Q$. If two representations have the same marginal law $Q$ and each requires its declared centre to be the unique Fréchet mean of that law, their centres are equal to $m_Q$. If the minimizer is nonunique, the law identifies the set $\mathfrak M(Q)=\operatorname{argmin}F_Q$, not a selected member.

### Proof

Equality of $Q$ makes the two Fréchet objectives identical. Each declared centre lies in the same argmin set. A singleton argmin forces equality. With multiple minimizers, the objective determines the whole set but contains no rule that distinguishes one element from another. ∎

The boundary is genuine. On the unit circle with intrinsic distance, put mass $1/2$ at each of two antipodes. On either semicircle parameterized by distance $x\in[0,\pi]$ from the first atom,
\[
F_Q(x)=\tfrac12x^2+\tfrac12(\pi-x)^2,
\]
whose minimum is at $x=\pi/2$. The two semicircles give two distinct midpoint minimizers. Thus uniqueness cannot be deleted from the conclusion.

### Assumptions, consumer, weakness

Finite second moment makes the displayed objective meaningful. Existence and uniqueness are assumptions here, not consequences of completeness alone. This closes the A-side audit of G1-0 and redirects curved work to weakened reference curves rather than two distinct unique means of one known law.

## 3. Claim A2 — necessary-and-sufficient flat decomposition quotient

### 3.1 Admissible second-order model

An orthogonal-white representation of a centered weakly stationary $H$-valued process is a tuple
\[
(r,A,f,\delta),\qquad X_t=Af_t+\delta_t,
\]
with:

1. $r<\infty$ and $A:\mathbb R^r\to H$ injective;
2. $f_t$ centered, square-integrable, and weakly stationary;
3. $\delta_t$ centered and weakly stationary with $\Gamma_\delta(h)=0$ for every $h\ne0$;
4. both cross directions vanish at every integer lag:
\[
\mathbb E[f_{t+h}\otimes\delta_t]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E[\delta_{t+h}\otimes f_t]=0.
\]

Calling $\delta$ “white” here means temporally uncorrelated. It does not mean independent unless Gaussian independence is separately imposed.

### 3.2 Second-order classification theorem

Two admissible tuples $(A,f,\delta)$ and $(B,g,\varepsilon)$ generate the same mean and covariance sequence if and only if
\[
A\Gamma_f(h)A^*=B\Gamma_g(h)B^*=\Gamma_X(h),\qquad h\ne0,
\tag{3.1}
\]
and
\[
A\Gamma_f(0)A^*+D_\delta
=B\Gamma_g(0)B^*+D_\varepsilon
=\Gamma_X(0),
\tag{3.2}
\]
where $D_\delta=\Gamma_\delta(0)$ and $D_\varepsilon=\Gamma_\varepsilon(0)$.

### Proof

Expanding $\Gamma_X(h)$, the two cross terms vanish by condition 4. For $h\ne0$, the noise term also vanishes, giving (3.1); for $h=0$, it gives (3.2). These conditions are necessary. Conversely, if admissible latent processes have covariance sequences satisfying (3.1)–(3.2), expansion gives the same mean and covariance sequence, proving sufficiency for second-order equivalence. ∎

### 3.3 Gaussian full-FDD classification

Restrict further to centered jointly Gaussian latent processes, with $f$ independent of the entire noise sequence and $\delta_t$ iid Gaussian. Then (3.1)–(3.2), together with positive definiteness of the proposed factor covariance sequence and positive semidefiniteness of the noise covariance, are necessary and sufficient for equality of all FDDs of the observed process. In infinite-dimensional $H$, the noise covariance is also trace class: from (3.2), $0\preceq D_\delta\preceq\Gamma_X(0)$ (and likewise for every alternative), while the covariance of a square-integrable $H$-valued variable is trace class.

### Proof

Necessity follows from the previous expansion. For sufficiency, construct independent Gaussian processes with the proposed covariance sequences. Their sums are centered Gaussian. Equations (3.1)–(3.2) give identical covariance between every pair of coordinates and times. A centered Gaussian FDD is uniquely determined by its covariance matrix, so every observed FDD is identical. ∎

This is an exact full-law quotient on the declared Gaussian class. Without Gaussianity, the equations are not promoted beyond second order: §1 gives a concrete failure of that promotion.

### 3.4 Exact white-at-zero reallocation

Suppose two minimum representations have $B=AR$ for an invertible $R$ (proved in §4). Put
\[
\bar\Gamma_f(h)=R^{-1}\Gamma_f(h)R^{-*}.
\]
Then every Gaussian full-law-equivalent minimum representation, and only such a representation, has
\[
\Gamma_g(h)=\bar\Gamma_f(h)\quad(h\ne0),\qquad
\Gamma_g(0)=\bar\Gamma_f(0)+K,
\tag{3.3}
\]
\[
D_\varepsilon=D_\delta-ARKR^*A^*,
\tag{3.4}
\]
for a self-adjoint $K$ such that the entire sequence in (3.3) is positive definite and $D_\varepsilon\succeq0$. Equations (3.3)–(3.4) are necessary by subtracting (3.1)–(3.2), and sufficient by the Gaussian theorem. Thus dynamically white variation in the loading space is not assigned uniquely between factors and idiosyncratic noise, even under full observed FDD information.

An explicit realization is $X_t=A(s_t+w_t)+e_t$, where $s$ is persistent Gaussian, while $w_t$ and $e_t$ are mutually independent iid Gaussian sequences independent of $s$. Both
\[
f_t=s_t+w_t,\;\delta_t=e_t
\quad\text{and}\quad
g_t=s_t,\;\varepsilon_t=Aw_t+e_t
\]
obey the model and produce the same observed process. This is factor/noise non-identification, not centre non-identification.

The same quotient has a continuum of exact minimum-rank representations. Fix the spectral convention
\[
\gamma(h)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{ih\lambda}g(\lambda)\,d\lambda.
\tag{3.5}
\]
Let the scalar observed process be centered stationary Gaussian with spectral density
$g(\lambda)=2+\cos\lambda$. For every $c\in(0,1)$, let
$f^{(c)}$ have spectral density $g-c$, let $\delta_t^{(c)}$ be iid
$N(0,c)$, and take the two processes independent. Since
$g-c\ge 1-c>0$, the factor covariance sequence is admissible; since a
constant spectral density $c$ has covariance $c\mathbf 1_{\{h=0\}}$,
the sum $f_t^{(c)}+\delta_t^{(c)}$ has density $g$ for every $c$.
Moreover, $\gamma_{f^{(c)}}(1)=1/2\ne0$, so every factor remains
genuinely dynamic and the loading rank remains the minimum value one.
Thus neither full Gaussian FDDs nor minimum dynamic rank select $c$.

### Assumptions, consumer, weakness

Claim A2 supplies ID-2. The two-sided cross-lag condition is load-bearing: if colored idiosyncratic components are admitted, $X_t=e_1z_t$ with persistent $z_t$ can be represented either as a factor with zero noise or as colored “noise” with no factor. Thus the persistent span is not identified in that enlarged class. Independence is load-bearing only for the stated Gaussian latent class; the broader second-order result requires orthogonality, not independence.

## 4. Claim A3 — minimum dynamic rank and the loading-span gauge

Define the observed dynamic span
\[
\mathcal S_X
=\overline{\operatorname{span}}
\{\operatorname{ran}\Gamma_X(h):h\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Including signed lags automatically includes ranges of adjoints. Assume $q=\dim\mathcal S_X<\infty$.

For a factor process define
\[
\mathcal D_f=\operatorname{span}
\{\operatorname{ran}\Gamma_f(h):h\ne0\}\subseteq\mathbb R^r.
\]

### Theorem

For every admissible representation,
\[
\mathcal S_X=A\mathcal D_f\subseteq\operatorname{ran}A.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Consequently $r\ge q$. Equality $r=q$ holds exactly when $\mathcal D_f=\mathbb R^r$, and then
\[
\operatorname{ran}A=\mathcal S_X.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Thus the minimum-dynamic-rank loading span is identified from all nonzero lag covariances. If $A$ and $B$ are two minimum representations, there is a unique $R\in GL(q)$ with $B=AR$; their nonzero-lag factor covariance sequences obey
\[
\Gamma_g(h)=R^{-1}\Gamma_f(h)R^{-*},\qquad h\ne0.
\tag{4.4}
\]
If $A^*A=B^*B=I_q$, then $R$ is orthogonal. Factor coordinates are therefore a $GL(q)$ gauge, or an orthogonal gauge after isometric normalization.

### Proof

By (3.1), $\Gamma_X(h)=A\Gamma_f(h)A^*$. Because $A$ is injective and finite-dimensional, $A^*:H\to\mathbb R^r$ is surjective. Hence
\[
\operatorname{ran}(A\Gamma_f(h)A^*)=A\operatorname{ran}\Gamma_f(h).
\]
Taking spans proves (4.2). Injectivity preserves dimension, so $q=\dim\mathcal D_f\le r$, with equality exactly when $\mathcal D_f=\mathbb R^r$. This yields (4.3).

If both representations are minimum, their ranges equal the same $q$-dimensional space. There is a unique invertible change of coordinates $R$ with $B=AR$. Substitute in (3.1) and left-invert $A$ and right-invert $A^*$ to obtain (4.4). Under isometric normalization,
$I=B^*B=R^*A^*AR=R^*R$, so $R$ is orthogonal. ∎

### Complementary rank-deficient lags

No single lag need have rank $q$. Let $f_{1,t}=a_t+\theta a_{t-1}$ and $f_{2,t}=b_t+\eta b_{t-2}$ for independent iid centered noises and nonzero $\theta,\eta$. Then $\Gamma_f(1)$ has range $\operatorname{span}(e_1)$, $\Gamma_f(2)$ has range $\operatorname{span}(e_2)$, and the combined dynamic span is $\mathbb R^2$. A theorem based on one selected lag would undercount the minimum rank; (4.1) does not.

### Dynamically silent directions

If a representation adds an independent iid factor $w_t$ loaded along a new direction, that direction appears only at lag zero and is absent from $\mathcal S_X$. Moving $Aw_t$ to the white noise leaves the observed law unchanged in the Gaussian construction of §3.4. Hence a loading space strictly larger than $\mathcal S_X$ is not identified without an additional convention that itself allocates white variation. Minimum dynamic rank is not aesthetic; it removes exactly these observationally silent directions.

### Frozen locally stationary extension

At local time $u$, apply the same proof to the frozen covariance $\Gamma_X(u,h)$. For a loading constrained to be common in $u$, the identified minimum common span is
\[
\mathcal S_{X,\mathrm{global}}
=\operatorname{span}_{u\in[0,1],\,h\ne0}\operatorname{ran}\Gamma_X(u,h),
\]
provided this span is finite-dimensional and the declared model admits a common loading. This is a population statement; recovering the continuum of frozen covariances from one path is separate.

### Assumptions, consumer, weakness

Claim A3 supplies the loading target for ID-2. It uses every signed nonzero lag. With only a finite lag set $\mathcal H$, the same proof identifies only $\operatorname{span}_{h\in\mathcal H}\operatorname{ran}\Gamma_X(h)$; equality with the full dynamic span is an additional rank condition, not automatic.

## 5. Claim A4 — deterministic changes of reference and what “absorption” means

Consider the exact identity
\[
X_t=m(u_t)+Af_t+\delta_t
=\{m(u_t)+Ag(u_t)\}+A\{f_t-g(u_t)\}+\delta_t,
\tag{5.1}
\]
where $g:[0,1]\to\mathbb R^r$ is deterministic and $A$ is injective.

### Classification

1. **Pure algebra / weakened reference:** (5.1) holds for every deterministic $g$ for which both reference curves are admitted. Thus a reference component in $\operatorname{ran}A$ can always be moved into uncentered scores algebraically.
2. **Pointwise centered factors:** if the model requires $\mathbb Ef_t=0$ at every local time in both representations, then $\mathbb E[f_t-g(u_t)]=-g(u_t)$; injectivity and centring force $g\equiv0$. If $m(u)$ is also the marginal mean, this agrees with A1.
3. **Weakly stationary factors with an unrestricted constant mean:** if $f_t$ is weakly stationary, then $f_t-g(u_t)$ is weakly stationary exactly when $g(u_t)$ is constant on the time index, because weak stationarity requires a constant mean and deterministic subtraction leaves covariance unchanged.
4. **Locally stationary factors allowing a smooth local mean:** absorption is admissible exactly for those $g$ satisfying the declared local-mean regularity. This is a model convention; imposing a zero frozen mean again forces $g=0$.
5. **Random invariant component:** a time-constant random variable $Z$ cannot be inserted into a deterministic centre curve. It may be reallocated among latent random components when the model allows it, but doing so is factor/noise or random-intercept non-identification, not ambiguity of a deterministic Fréchet mean.
6. **Outside-loading drift:** a change $d(u)$ can be absorbed with the fixed loading $A$ if and only if $d(u)\in\operatorname{ran}A$ pointwise. Necessity follows from $d=Ag$; sufficiency uses the unique coefficient $g=(A^*A)^{-1}A^*d$.

### Proof

Only items 3 and 6 need more than taking expectations in (5.1). For item 3, the new mean is the old constant mean minus $g(u_t)$, so it is constant if and only if $g(u_t)$ is constant; the covariance is unchanged by deterministic subtraction. For item 6, injectivity makes $A^*A$ invertible, and the displayed coefficient is the inverse of $A$ on its range. ∎

### Assumptions, consumer, weakness

This closes the exact flat change-of-reference classification used by ID-2 and delimits ID-4. “Absorption” alone never proves that the alternative remains in a centered, stationary, or unique-mean model. A random shift is deliberately excluded from the deterministic-centre claim.

## 6. Claim A5 — invariant components, zero frequency, and pointwise mean ergodicity

Let $Z_t$ be centered, square-integrable, weakly stationary and $H$-valued. Let $F_Z$ be its finite positive operator-valued spectral measure and let
$\nu(B)=\operatorname{tr}F_Z(B)$, a finite scalar measure. Define
\[
\bar Z_k=\frac1k\sum_{t=1}^k Z_t,
\qquad
D_k(\lambda)=\frac1k\sum_{t=1}^ke^{it\lambda}.
\]

### Theorem

There is a time-invariant random component $Z^{\mathrm{inv}}$ such that
\[
\bar Z_k\longrightarrow Z^{\mathrm{inv}}\quad\text{in }L^2(H),
\qquad
\mathbb E\|Z^{\mathrm{inv}}\|^2=\nu(\{0\}).
\tag{6.1}
\]
Consequently
\[
\bar Z_k\to0\text{ in }L^2(H)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
F_Z(\{0\})=0.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Thus absence of a zero-frequency atom is necessary and sufficient for pointwise $L^2$ recovery of the deterministic mean by ordinary averaging within this stationary finite-second-moment class.

### Proof

On the Hilbert space generated in $L^2(\Omega;H)$ by the coordinates of $Z$, the time shift is unitary. The Hilbert-space mean ergodic theorem gives
\[
k^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^kU^tZ_0\to P_{\mathrm{Fix}(U)}Z_0=:Z^{\mathrm{inv}}
\]
in $L^2(H)$, and $U Z^{\mathrm{inv}}=Z^{\mathrm{inv}}$. Equivalently, by the spectral representation,
\[
\mathbb E\|\bar Z_k\|^2
=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|D_k(\lambda)|^2\,\nu(d\lambda).
\]
Here $|D_k|\le1$, $D_k(0)=1$, and $D_k(\lambda)\to0$ for every $\lambda\ne0$. Dominated convergence yields the limit $\nu(\{0\})$, which is the squared norm of the fixed-space projection. A positive operator has zero trace exactly when it is zero, proving (6.2). ∎

### Exact taxonomy

- A deterministic time-varying curve $m(u)$ is a mean/reference term, not a spectral component of a stationary centered residual.
- A deterministic constant belongs to the mean before centring.
- A centered random constant $Z_t=W$ is invariant and has all its spectral mass at zero; one-path averages converge to $W$, not zero.
- A general zero-frequency atom is exactly the $L^2$ invariant projection, whether or not the process was introduced by the modeller as a “random intercept.”
- Continuous spectral mass arbitrarily near zero has no invariant component, so pointwise averaging is consistent, but §8 proves that recovery is not uniform over this qualitative class.

### Assumptions, consumer, weakness

Claim A5 supplies the exact pointwise boundary for ID-3. It concerns an expanding stationary window. It gives no rate without quantitative control of spectral mass near zero.

## 7. Claim A6 — transfer to a local triangular-array window

Fix $u\in(0,1)$. Let $W_n(u)$ be a consecutive index window of size $k_n\to\infty$ with
\[
\max_{t\in W_n(u)}|t/n-u|\to0.
\tag{7.1}
\]
Suppose
\[
X_{t,n}=m(t/n)+Z^{(u)}_{t-t_n}+r_{t,n}^{(u)},
\]
where $m$ is continuous at $u$, $Z^{(u)}$ is a centered stationary $H$-valued process, and
\[
\sup_{t\in W_n(u)}\mathbb E\|r_{t,n}^{(u)}\|^2\to0.
\tag{7.2}
\]
Then the local average $\widehat m_n(u)=k_n^{-1}\sum_{t\in W_n(u)}X_{t,n}$ satisfies
\[
\widehat m_n(u)\to m(u)\text{ in }L^2(H)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
F_{Z^{(u)}}(\{0\})=0,
\tag{7.3}
\]
provided the approximation remainder and deterministic bias in (7.1)–(7.2) vanish as stated. More precisely, if an invariant component exists, the limit is $m(u)+Z^{(u),\mathrm{inv}}$, so recovery of $m(u)$ fails unless that component is zero.

### Proof

Decompose
\[
\widehat m_n(u)-m(u)
=\frac1{k_n}\sum_{t\in W_n(u)}[m(t/n)-m(u)]
+\frac1{k_n}\sum_{j=1}^{k_n}Z_j^{(u)}
+\frac1{k_n}\sum_{t\in W_n(u)}r_{t,n}^{(u)}.
\]
The first term tends to zero deterministically by continuity and (7.1). Jensen's inequality bounds the squared $L^2$ norm of the last term by the supremum in (7.2). Claim A5 gives convergence of the middle term to $Z^{(u),\mathrm{inv}}$ and makes its vanishing equivalent to no zero atom. ∎

### Assumptions, consumer, weakness

This is the exact transfer theorem consumed by ID-3. It does not claim that every published definition of local stationarity implies (7.2); the canonical integration must verify that implication for whichever array class it adopts. Boundary points require one-sided windows but the same proof.

## 8. Claim A7 — qualitative no-atom assumptions do not permit uniform recovery

### Statement

Over the class of scalar stationary Gaussian processes with unknown constant mean, unit marginal variance, and no spectral atom at zero, no estimator of the mean based on $n$ consecutive observations is uniformly consistent. In particular, no uniform rate follows from the qualitative no-atom assumption.

### Proof

Consider Gaussian AR(1) laws with covariance
$\Sigma_\rho(i,j)=\rho^{|i-j|}$, $0<\rho<1$. Every such law has an absolutely continuous spectral density and hence no zero-frequency atom. For the $n$-vector, direct multiplication by the tridiagonal AR(1) precision matrix gives
\[
I_n(\rho):=\mathbf1^T\Sigma_\rho^{-1}\mathbf1
=\frac{n(1-\rho)+2\rho}{1+\rho}.
\tag{8.1}
\]
Choose $\rho_n=1-n^{-2}$. Then $I_n(\rho_n)\le3/2$ for $n\ge2$. Compare the two admissible laws with means $+a$ and $-a$, the same $\Sigma_{\rho_n}$, and fixed $a=1/4$. Their Kullback–Leibler divergence is
\[
\mathrm{KL}(P_{+a}\|P_{-a})
=\tfrac12(2a)^2I_n(\rho_n)\le3a^2.
\]
Pinsker's inequality gives
$\|P_{+a}-P_{-a}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le a\sqrt{3/2}<1$.
For any estimator $\widehat\mu_n$, choosing the sign of $\widehat\mu_n$ as a test shows
\[
\sup_{\mu\in\{-a,a\}}\mathbb E_\mu(\widehat\mu_n-\mu)^2
\ge \frac{a^2}{2}
\{1-\|P_{+a}-P_{-a}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\}>0,
\tag{8.2}
\]
uniformly in $n$. Since the supremum over the full no-atom class is at least this two-point risk, uniform consistency is impossible. ∎

The same obstruction applies to a local window after replacing $n$ by its effective size $k_n$. Each fixed $\rho<1$ is pointwise ergodic and permits consistency; the adversarial sequence $\rho_{k_n}\uparrow1$ demonstrates ill-conditioning, not population non-identification.

An exact spectral illustration reaches the same boundary for the ordinary
average without using a continuous spectrum. Under the convention
$\gamma(h)=\int e^{ih\lambda}F(d\lambda)$, let
\[
Z_t^{(\epsilon)}=U\cos(\epsilon t)+V\sin(\epsilon t),
\]
where $U,V$ are independent $N(0,\sigma^2)$. This is strictly
stationary Gaussian because it is centered Gaussian with covariance
$\gamma_\epsilon(h)=\sigma^2\cos(\epsilon h)$. Its spectral measure is
\[
F_\epsilon=\frac{\sigma^2}{2}
(\delta_{\epsilon}+\delta_{-\epsilon}),
\]
so for every $\epsilon\ne0$ it has atoms near zero but none at zero. Its
sample-average variance is
\[
\mathbb E\left|\frac1N\sum_{t=1}^NZ_t^{(\epsilon)}\right|^2
=\sigma^2\left|\frac1N\sum_{t=1}^Ne^{it\epsilon}\right|^2.
\tag{8.3}
\]

## 9. Claim A8 — exact aligned, orthogonal, partial, and invisible drift examples

For these examples let $f_t$ be a centered stationary scalar Gaussian process with $\mathbb E[f_{t+h}f_t]=\rho^h$, $0<\rho<1$, and let white noise be zero for simplicity. A fixed-centre fit at zero observes
\[
Y_t=d_t+e_1f_t.
\]
For constant deterministic $d_t=d$, the exact uncentered population lag moment is
\[
M(h)=\mathbb E[Y_{t+h}\otimes Y_t]
=\rho^h e_1\otimes e_1+d\otimes d.
\tag{9.1}
\]
The drift–factor cross terms vanish here because $f_t$ is pointwise centered; they do not vanish in a model with nonzero factor local means or stochastic drift correlated with factors.

1. **Aligned drift.** In $H=\mathbb R^2$, take $d=ce_1$. Then
\[
M(h)=(\rho^h+c^2)e_1\otimes e_1.
\]
Drift changes the eigenvalue but neither rank nor loading span.

2. **Orthogonal drift.** Take $d=ce_2$. Then
\[
M(h)=\rho^h e_1\otimes e_1+c^2e_2\otimes e_2,
\]
which has rank two for $c\ne0$. Drift adds a direction orthogonal to the true factor loading.

3. **Partly aligned drift.** Take $d=c(e_1+e_2)$. In the basis $(e_1,e_2)$,
\[
M(h)=
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho^h+c^2&c^2\\
c^2&c^2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det M(h)=c^2\rho^h>0.
\]
The rank rises to two and the empirical eigendirections mix the factor and drift directions. The output does not label either contribution.

4. **Invisibility at a selected lag.** For a fixed $h_0\ge1$, let
$d_t=c\cos(\pi t/(2h_0))e_2$ and define the time-averaged drift row by its Cesàro limit. Then
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\sum_{t=1}^N d_{t+h_0}\otimes d_t=0
\]
because the average of $-\sin(\pi t/(2h_0))\cos(\pi t/(2h_0))$ is zero. Hence drift can be invisible to one selected lag while visible at others. A finite-lag rank claim therefore needs an explicit drift-row rank condition.

5. **Partial absorption boundary.** For a general $d=d_\parallel+d_\perp$ with $d_\parallel\in\operatorname{ran}A$ and $d_\perp\perp\operatorname{ran}A$, §5 absorbs exactly $d_\parallel$ in a weakened-reference model. No coefficient change with the fixed $A$ absorbs $d_\perp$. Equation (9.1) shows that the fixed-centre lag object nonetheless superposes both parts and their cross operators through $d\otimes d$.

These exact examples establish “can superpose,” “can add rank,” “can leave the span unchanged,” and “can be invisible to selected lags.” They do not establish that drift dominates Factor 1 in any dataset.
