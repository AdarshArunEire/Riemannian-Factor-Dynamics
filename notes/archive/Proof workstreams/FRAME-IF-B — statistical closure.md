# FRAME-IF-B — statistical closure

> The FRAME-DB brief and all archived FRAME-DB A/B/C/lead records were reread. This is a frozen hostile-audit candidate, not canon.

## 0. Final verdict

The identically smoothed, score-only FRAME-IF construction is **DISPROVED** because validation scores centred at the same smoothed barycentres cannot see the pilot's third-order bias. It is not a generic impossibility. An independent validation mean with bandwidth \(c_n=n^{-\gamma}\), \(1/6<\gamma<3/14\), estimates the pilot's full realized path error. Differentiating the evaluation polygon row toward that validation path cancels the pilot first order; validation bias, aggregate fluctuation, and nonlinear remainder are respectively \(o(n^{-1/2})\), \(O_p(n^{-1/2})\), and \(o(n^{-1/2})\).

The completion audit in §11 promotes the two-path correction to Gate A under explicit dimension-uniform strong-convexity, composed-action, Richardson/Jacobi, replacement, mask, and dependence producers. A feasible finite-dimensional smooth-nuisance one-step theorem remains a separate fallback.

## 1. Target, row norm, and masks

Let \(H_0=T_{\mu_{\theta_0}(u_0)}M\), \(h_0,m_0,k,p<\infty\), and

\[
Z_{t,h}(\theta)=Y_t(\theta)\otimes Y_{t-h}(\theta)\in\mathcal S_2(H_0),
\quad
\mathcal T_I(\theta,P)=\bigoplus_{h=1}^{h_0}|I_h|^{-1}\sum_{t\in I_h}E_PZ_{t,h}(\theta).
\tag{1.1}
\]

All anchor fibres are identified by the radial connector from the observable pilot anchor. A common anchor rotation conjugates every block and is quotiented out. The norm is

\[
\|B\|_{\oplus HS}^2=\sum_{h=1}^{h_0}\|B_h\|_{HS}^2.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Partition time into periodic superblocks. Each superblock contains training, validation, and evaluation cores \(T,V,E\), separated on both sides by \(g=m_0+h_0\) deleted indices. Use only within-core lag pairs. The three sigma-fields are generated by disjoint innovation sets. Require \(|T|,|V|,|E|\asymp n\). In the theorem the pulled-back tangent row is stationary, so all three periodic masks target the same \(\Gamma_h\). For a nonstationary target, the theorem instead targets the declared evaluation mask \(I_h=E_h\); comparison to another mask is the explicit first-order defect \(\rho_{mask,n}\).

## 2. Disproof of canonical nonparametric FRAME-IF

### 2.1 Population smoothing bias

Let \(\mu_{c,b}(u)\) be the population one-sided positive-weight barycentre at scale \(cb\), and let \(\mu_b^R(u)\) be the canonical tangent Richardson combination with

\[
c=(1,1/2,1/4),\qquad \lambda=(1/3,-2,8/3).
\]

For a \(C^3\) regular law path, normal-coordinate barycentre expansion gives

\[
e_b^R(u):=\log_{\mu(u)}\mu_b^R(u)
=b^3B_3(u)+o(b^3),
\tag{2.1}
\]

whenever the third coefficient is nonzero. The scale multiplier cannot vanish because

\[
\sum_{j=1}^3\lambda_jc_j^3
=\frac13-\frac14+\frac1{24}=\frac18.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Thus the three-scale system cancels orders one and two, not order three. The canonical assumptions bound \(B_3\); they do not set it to zero.

### 2.2 Why validation scores cannot see this bias

For stage \(j\), let \(\Psi_{j,b}(q)=E\sum_iw_{j,i}(u)\log_qX_i\). By definition

\[
\Psi_{j,b}\{\mu_{c_j,b}(u)\}=0.
\tag{2.3}
\]

An independent validation copy with identical weights and mask has the same conditional population score. It estimates stochastic displacement from a smoothed population barycentre; it has zero population information about \(\log_{\mu(u)}\mu_{c_j,b}(u)\). Combining stage scores with \(\lambda_j\) preserves (2.3). Consequently the sign-correct score correction

\[
T(\widehat\mu^R)+\widehat K\widehat A^{-1}\widehat\Psi^V
\tag{2.4}
\]

has, even with oracle \(K,A\) and infinite validation data,

\[
E\{(2.4)\}-T\{\mu(P)\}
=b^3K[B_3]+o(b^3)+O(Mb^6+M^{-2}).
\tag{2.5}
\]

The direct lag-law empirical influence is irrelevant to the deterministic term in (2.5).

### 2.3 Nonzero curved coefficient

Take \(M=\mathbb H^2(-1)\), a small convex normal ball about \(o\), and in normal coordinates choose

\[
\mu(u)=\operatorname{Exp}_o\{Lu\,e_1+\gamma u^3e_2\},\qquad L\gamma\ne0,
\tag{2.6}
\]

with \(L,\gamma\) small enough for the generated tube. Use a centred simultaneously sign-symmetric bounded tangent lag process with

\[
\Gamma(1)=c_f\,e_1\otimes e_1,\qquad c_f\ne0.
\tag{2.7}
\]

At \(\gamma=0\), the third one-sided design moment and (2.2) give a nonzero transverse component of \(B_3\) after differentiating in \(\gamma\); continuity preserves it for small nonzero \(\gamma\). Constant curvature gives

\[
\Omega_{B_3}(u)=\int_0^uP_{s\to0}R\{B_3(s),\dot\mu(s)\}P_{0\to s}\,ds,
\tag{2.8}
\]

which is a nonzero skew generator because \(B_3\wedge\dot\mu\ne0\). A nonzero planar skew does not commute with (2.7), so a retained interior interval can be chosen on which

\[
\left\|\int\{\Omega_{B_3}(u)\Gamma(1)-\Gamma(1)\Omega_{B_3}(u-h/n)\}\,du\right\|_{HS}>0.
\tag{2.9}
\]

The DGP can be one-dependent and smooth, as in Section 5.

### 2.4 Rate contradiction

At \(b_n=n^{-1/7}\), (2.5)--(2.9) give a direct-sum HS bias bounded below by \(c n^{-3/7}\). FRAME-IF-POLY asserts only

\[
M_n\ell_n^2+M_n^{-2}+O(\ell_n^2)+o(n^{-1/2}),
\quad M_n\asymp\ell_n^{-2/3},\quad \ell_n=n^{-3/7},
\tag{2.10}
\]

whose polygon term is \(\ell_n^{4/3}=n^{-4/7}=o(n^{-1/2})\). The omitted \(b_n^3\) term is larger. Therefore the identically smoothed score-only lemma is **DISPROVED**. The following undersmoothed two-path construction defeats this obstruction.

## 2A. Feasible undersmoothed two-path correction

Use the separated colours \(T,V,E\) and vertices \(u_j=j/M_n\). On \(T\), compute canonical positive three-scale pilot vertices \(\widehat q_j\) at \(b_n=n^{-1/7}\). On \(V\), compute independent positive three-scale vertices \(\check q_j\) at \(c_n=n^{-\gamma}\), \(1/6<\gamma<3/14\). Put
\[
d_j=\log_{\widehat q_j}\check q_j,\qquad
q_j(s)=\operatorname{Exp}_{\widehat q_j}(sd_j).
\tag{2A.1}
\]
Here “positive three-scale” means that each of the three constituent smoothers is a positive-weight empirical barycentre, followed by the declared signed Richardson post-map in one fixed tangent fibre. The post-map and its first two derivatives have a uniform \(C^2\) norm on the generated compact tube; its fixed weights have bounded \(\ell^1\) norm. Thus barycentre replacement bounds pass to the Richardson output by the chain rule. This is an explicit producer assumption, not a consequence of positive weights alone.

Join \(q_j(s)\) by geodesic cells and let \(\widehat{\mathcal T}_E\{q(s)\}\) be the evaluation polygon row. Define
\[
\boxed{\widehat{\mathcal T}^{2p}
=\widehat{\mathcal T}_E\{\widehat q\}
+\left.\frac d{ds}\widehat{\mathcal T}_E\{q(s)\}\right|_{s=0}.}
\tag{2A.2}
\]
This exact observable derivative includes base-log Hessians, completed/partial cell Jacobi terms, endpoint connectors, and anchor synchronization. It uses no true geometric object.

All rows and derivatives use within-core lag pairs, gaps at least \(m_0+h_0\), and the identical deterministic mask and masked target on \(T,V,E\). Let \(K_{n,j}\) be the population derivative of that masked row with respect to vertex \(j\). Represent the whole derivative in \(T_{\widehat q_0}M\); when the anchor moves, identify \(T_{q_0(s)}M\) with this fixed fibre by the radial connector. Its first-order generator is zero. Differentiation then leaves only the first-cell curvature integral and the base-log contribution of observations in that cell, both of row mass \(O(M_n^{-1})\); the terminal connector cancels the next cell's initial connector. Hence the anchor need not be frozen and \(\|K_{n,0}\|\le C/M_n\). The same two-cell cancellation applies internally and at the terminal vertex. Uniform Jacobi/base-log bounds therefore give
\[
\max_j\|K_{n,j}\|\le C/M_n,\qquad \sum_j\|K_{n,j}\|\le C.
\tag{2A.3}
\]
One validation observation occurs in \(O(M_nc_n)\) vertex windows and has barycentre influence \(O((nc_n)^{-1})\) at each. Its aggregate coefficient is
\[
O(M_nc_n)O(M_n^{-1})O((nc_n)^{-1})=O(n^{-1}).
\tag{2A.4}
\]
Finite-memory colouring and the Hilbert/HS Efron--Stein inequality hence give aggregate validation fluctuation \(O_p(n^{-1/2})\), without a \(\sqrt M\) loss. For the nonlinear remainder, impose uniform second replacement stability of every constituent barycentre and of the fixed-fibre Richardson post-map. If validation observations \(i,k\) have overlapping kernel windows, the vertex second difference is \(C/(n^2c_n^2)\); summing over their \(O(M_nc_n)\) common affected vertices with action \(C/M_n\) gives
\[
\|\Delta_i\Delta_k\widehat{\mathcal T}^{2p}\|_{\oplus HS}
\le {C\over n^2c_n}.
\tag{2A.4a}
\]
Nonoverlapping windows have zero second difference, apart from the fixed-memory enlargement, and there are \(O(n^2c_n)\) overlapping pairs. The second-order Efron--Stein/ANOVA bound consequently yields the Hájek remainder
\[
\left\|F-EF-\sum_iF_i\right\|_{L^2(\oplus HS)}
\le C\{n^2c_n(n^2c_n)^{-2}\}^{1/2}
={C\over n\sqrt{c_n}}=o(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{2A.4b}
\]

Assume fixed \(p,h_0,m_0\), bounded energy, exact separation/GLO/lag factorisation, \(C^4\) law/mean smoothness, known geometry on a uniform compact generated tube, the displayed first/second replacement bounds, and derivatives through second polygon variation. Assume either exact local-law sampling, or the canonical coupling with exponent \(a>1/2\) and identical-mask/Riemann defect \(\rho_{mask,n}=o(n^{-1/2})\). Thus \(n^{-a}\) is explicitly sub-root-\(n\), not hidden in a residual. Let
\[
r_T=O_p(\ell_n),\qquad r_V=O_p\{c_n^3+(nc_n)^{-1/2}\}
\tag{2A.5}
\]
be vertex RMS errors, with the arbitrary-grid maximum theorem supplying the tube. Taylor expansion gives
\[
\widehat{\mathcal T}^{2p}-\mathcal T
=\mathbb G_E[Z]+\mathbb G_V[\varphi_c]
+O_p\{c_n^3+M_n(r_T^2+r_Tr_V+r_V^2)+M_n^{-2}\}
+O_p\{(nc_n)^{-1}+(n\sqrt{c_n})^{-1}\}
+O(n^{-a})+\rho_{mask,n}.
\tag{2A.6}
\]
After connectors,
\[
d_j=e_{V,j}-e_{T,j}+O(\|e_{T,j}\|^2+\|e_{V,j}\|^2),
\tag{2A.7}
\]
so \(K[e_T]+K[d]=K[e_V]\): the pilot first order cancels. Validation bias is not multiplied by \(M_n\), because \(\sum_j\|K_{n,j}\|\le C\).

For \(\ell_n=n^{-3/7}\), \(M_n=n^{2/7}\), \(c_n=n^{-\gamma}\),
\[
c_n^3=o(n^{-1/2})\iff\gamma>1/6,\quad
M_n/(nc_n)=o(n^{-1/2})\iff\gamma<3/14,
\tag{2A.8}
\]
while \(M_nc_n^6=o(n^{-1/2})\), \(M_n\ell_n^2+M_n^{-2}=2n^{-4/7}\), and
\[
M_n\ell_n(nc_n)^{-1/2}
=n^{-9/14+\gamma/2}=o(n^{-1/2}),\qquad
M_n\ell_nc_n^3=n^{-1/7-3\gamma}=o(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{2A.8a}
\]
Also
\[
(nc_n)^{-1}=n^{-1+\gamma}=o(n^{-1/2}),\qquad
(n\sqrt{c_n})^{-1}=n^{-1+\gamma/2}=o(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{2A.8b}
\]
The first relation in (2A.8a) follows from \(\gamma<3/14<2/7\), and the second from \(\gamma>1/6>5/42\); (2A.8b) follows throughout the same window. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\|\widehat{\mathcal T}^{2p}-\mathcal T\|_{\oplus HS}=O_p(n^{-1/2}),}
\qquad d_{F,db,n}=o_p(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{2A.9}
\]
The validation influence is part of the leading row, not the nuisance remainder.

## 3. Restricted feasible complete one-step estimator

### Assumptions IF-P

1. \(M\) and its Levi--Civita operations are known on a fixed generated convex tube. The dimension \(p\), nuisance dimension \(k\), \(h_0\), and \(m_0\) are fixed.
2. The mean path is \(\mu_\theta(u)\), \(\theta\in\Theta\subset\mathbb R^k\), \(C^3\) jointly in \((u,\theta)\). Its smooth or deterministic \(M_n\)-cell polygon frame and derivatives through order two are computable from \(\theta\).
3. There is a bounded estimating score \(g_t(\theta)\in\mathbb R^k\), satisfying
   \[
   Eg_t(\theta_0)=0,\qquad D_\theta Eg_t(\theta_0)=-J,
   \quad 0<c_J\le s_{min}(J)\le\|J\|\le C_J,
   \tag{3.1}
   \]
   with bounded second derivative. The training root solves \(\bar g_T(\widehat\theta)=0\) and obeys \(\widehat\theta-\theta_0=O_p(n^{-1/2})\).
4. \(Z_{t,h}(\theta)\), \(D_\theta Z_{t,h}(\theta)\), and \(D_\theta^2Z_{t,h}(\theta)\) are uniformly bounded in HS/multilinear norm. Put
   \[
   K_h=|E_h|^{-1}\sum_{t\in E_h}E D_\theta Z_{t,h}(\theta_0),
   \quad K=\oplus_hK_h:\mathbb R^k\to\oplus_h\mathcal S_2(H_0).
   \tag{3.2}
   \]
5. The exact three-colour construction of Section 1 holds. GLO and exact included-lag factor/noise orthogonality hold. Total tangent energy is bounded by \(R\).

### Computation

On \(T\), compute

\[
\widehat J_T=-|T|^{-1}\sum_{t\in T}D_\theta g_t(\widehat\theta),
\quad
\widehat K_T=\bigoplus_h|T_h|^{-1}\sum_{t\in T_h}D_\theta Z_{t,h}(\widehat\theta).
\tag{3.3}
\]

All \(Z,DZ\) use the same observable anchor \(\mu_{\widehat\theta}(u_0)\) and fitted frame; no truth alignment is computed. On \(V,E\), compute

\[
\bar g_V(\widehat\theta)=|V|^{-1}\sum_{t\in V}g_t(\widehat\theta),
\quad
\widehat{\mathcal T}_E(\widehat\theta)=\bigoplus_h|E_h|^{-1}\sum_{t\in E_h}Z_{t,h}(\widehat\theta).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Define

\[
\boxed{\widehat{\mathcal T}^{db}
=\widehat{\mathcal T}_E(\widehat\theta)
+\widehat K_T\widehat J_T^{-1}\bar g_V(\widehat\theta).}
\tag{3.5}
\]

The plus sign follows from \(D Eg=-J\). A common change of fitted anchor conjugates both terms, so (3.5) is gauge equivariant.

## 4. Direct-sum HS theorem

> **Theorem IF-P-ROW.** Under IF-P,
> \[
> \widehat{\mathcal T}^{db}-\mathcal T_E(\theta_0,P)
> =(P_E-P)Z(\theta_0)+KJ^{-1}(P_V-P)g(\theta_0)+R_n,
> \tag{4.1}
> \]
> where
> \[
> \|R_n\|_{\oplus HS}=O_p(n^{-1}+M_n n^{-1}+M_n^{-2})+\rho_{mask,n}.
> \tag{4.2}
> \]
> If the smooth frame is evaluated exactly, delete the \(M_n\) terms. If \(M_n\asymp n^{1/3}\), then
> \[
> \|R_n\|_{\oplus HS}=o_p(n^{-1/2})+\rho_{mask,n},
> \qquad
> \|\widehat{\mathcal T}^{db}-\mathcal T_E\|_{\oplus HS}=O_p(n^{-1/2})+\rho_{mask,n}.
> \tag{4.3}
> \]

**Proof.** Write \(\delta=\widehat\theta-\theta_0\). Conditional on \(T\), Taylor expansion on \(E\) gives

\[
\widehat{\mathcal T}_E(\widehat\theta)
=\mathcal T_E+(P_E-P)Z_0+K\delta
+O_p(n^{-1/2}\|\delta\|+\|\delta\|^2)+R_{poly,E}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Finite-memory HS concentration of \(D Z\) and \(\delta=O_p(n^{-1/2})\) make the stochastic product \(O_p(n^{-1})\). On \(V\),

\[
\bar g_V(\widehat\theta)
=(P_V-P)g_0-J\delta+O_p(n^{-1/2}\|\delta\|+\|\delta\|^2).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Training concentration and smooth inversion give

\[
\|\widehat K_T-K\|=O_p(n^{-1/2})+O_p(M_nn^{-1}+M_n^{-2}),
\quad \|\widehat J_T^{-1}-J^{-1}\|=O_p(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Substitute (4.5)--(4.6) into (3.5). The terms \(K\delta\) and \(-KJ^{-1}J\delta\) cancel. Every product of a training \(O_p(n^{-1/2})\) error with the validation \(O_p(n^{-1/2})\) score is \(O_p(n^{-1})\). For the polygon, uniform vertex perturbation is \(O_p(n^{-1/2})\), so

\[
M_n r_\theta^2+M_n^{-2}=O_p(M_nn^{-1}+M_n^{-2}).
\tag{4.7}
\]

The two leading sums in (4.1) are independent finite-memory averages in \(\oplus_h\mathcal S_2(H_0)\), hence \(O_p(n^{-1/2})\). This proves the theorem. \(\square\)

The empirical correction influence \(KJ^{-1}(P_V-P)g_0\) is part of the leading row. The frame-debiasing nuisance remainder is (4.2), not this root-\(n\) fluctuation.

## 5. Explicit nonempty curved DGP

Let \(M=\mathbb H^2(-1)\), fix \(o\) and \((e_1,e_2)\), and set

\[

\[
f_t=\xi_t+a\xi_{t-1},\qquad

\tag{5.2}

\[
X_t=\operatorname{Exp}_{\mu_{\theta_0}(u_t)}

The process is one-dependent. Simultaneous sign reversal preserves every lag-pair law; on constant curvature the observation Hessian is even in \(Y\), so GLO holds. The iid \(e_2\) noise has no positive-lag covariance or factor cross covariance. At lag one,

\[
\Gamma(1)=a\operatorname{Var}(\xi_0)e_1\otimes e_1,

The geodesic-fan calculation here still shows why \(T\wedge V\ne0\) yields a nonzero constant-curvature generator which does not commute with the rank-one row. ### 11.2 Pathwise barycentre replacements

Fix one stage, vertex, and bandwidth \(a=a_n\). Write its empirical score as
\[
G(q)=\sum_t w_t g(q,X_t),\qquad G(\widehat q)=0.
\]
Replace \(X_i\) by \(X_i'\). Strong monotonicity of \(G\), radial tangent-fibre comparison, and \(\|g(q,X_i')-g(q,X_i)\|\le 2R\) give
\[
d(\widehat q^{\,i},\widehat q)
\le {2R\over m}w_i\le {C\over na}.
\tag{11.5}
\]
This proof uses only a Hilbert norm and the scalar modulus \(m\), hence has no \(p_n\)-factor.

For distinct \(i,k\), interpolate their score contributions by scalar contamination parameters \(s,t\in[0,1]\). Every interpolated objective remains \(m\)-strongly convex. The implicit equation \(G_{s,t}(q_{s,t})=0\) gives
\[
D_s q=-H^{-1}D_sG,\qquad
D_tD_s q=-H^{-1}\{(D_tH)D_sq+D_qD_sG[D_tq]\}.
\tag{11.6}
\]
Here \(\|H^{-1}\|\le m^{-1}\), \(\|D_sG\|\le Cw_i\), \(\|D_tG\|\le Cw_k\), and every remaining derivative is bounded by \(L\). Integration over \([0,1]^2\) yields the pathwise mixed difference
\[
d_{\mathrm{fib}}\{\Delta_i\Delta_k\widehat q,0\}
\le Cw_iw_k\le {C\over n^2a^2}.
\tag{11.7}
\]
The same calculation applies after the three-stage Richardson map: its chain rule adds \(DR_n[\Delta_i\Delta_k q]\) and \(D^2R_n[\Delta_iq,\Delta_kq]\), both of the order in (11.7). Thus
\[
\|\Delta_i\widehat q^R_j\|\le {C\over na},\qquad
\|\Delta_i\Delta_k\widehat q^R_j\|\le {C\over n^2a^2},
\tag{11.8}
\]
uniformly in \(p_n\).

Dimension-free Hilbert concentration of the population-centred local score, followed by strong monotonicity and a union bound over \(M_n+1\) vertices, gives

Consequently the generated-tube event has probability tending to one without a \(\sqrt{p_n}\) term.
